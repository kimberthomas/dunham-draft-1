    About

    Katherine Dunham (1909–2006) was a dancer, choreographer, anthropologist, author, educator, and activist. She brought the dances of the African diaspora to concert stages around the world, and she treated dance as a form of knowledge.

    Much of that knowledge is preserved in the Katherine Dunham Collection at the Library of Congress. Its photographs and moving images show how her choreography, research, and activism were connected.

    "Project Title" invites you to explore that collection. Each point of light on the photograph opens an item from the archive. Touch them in any order, and the order you choose becomes a path across a stage floor that you can keep, or dance if you’d like.

    Your floor plan is your own path through the archive, not a Dunham dance.

    Jemima Wright and Ysanne Johnson, interns in the Library of Congress’s Archives, History, and Heritage Advanced (AHHA) program, created this project with mentorship from Senior Innovation Specialist Kimber Thomas, as part of the Connecting Communities Digital Initiative (CCDI). Through archival research, interpretive design, and digital humanities methods, they aim to make the Library’s collections more visible and to show how digital tools can bring unique historical records to a wider public.
